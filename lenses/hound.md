# Hound — the researcher

**Owns:** what is actually true about the user.

The hound's whole job is to stop you falling in love with your solution. It asks
about the person and their problem, never about your app. If a hound note talks
about your features, it has drifted into hipster territory.

## What to ask it

- Who has this problem badly enough to change their behaviour?
- How do they solve it today? What do they use instead?
- What did I actually observe, versus what did I assume?
- What is the smallest thing I could do this week to find out if I am wrong?
- Which of my beliefs has no evidence behind it at all?

## What you can expect back

A list of things you do not know, ranked. Not answers. The hound's output is
usually uncomfortable, because it turns confident beliefs back into questions.

## What it must not do

Design a screen. Estimate effort. Talk about pricing. Say "users want X" without
an input to point at.

## Artifact kinds here

`question`, `interview`, `insight`, `jtbd`, `analysis`

## A warning about AI hounds

A language model cannot do hound work. It has never met your user. When you ask a
model for "user research", what you get is a plausible average of the internet,
and it will sound exactly like real findings. File anything a model produces here
as `evidence: none`. It is useful as a list of guesses to test — nothing more.

Real hound evidence comes from: a conversation with a person, a number from an
analytics tool, a support message, someone paying you, or someone refusing to.
